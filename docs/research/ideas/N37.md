## 1) Spec recap (catalogue, verbatim key lines)

CAT line 12 (top-10 list):

> 4. **The 120-Metre Gap (N37).** Explains why renters (38.7% of Waterloo households) miss statutory notice, and drafts the written notice request that O. Reg. 545/06 s.5(8) allows. *(F3 · B5 · S4)*

CAT lines 353–358:

> **N37 · The 120-Metre Gap (Renter Relay).** Shows who the law notifies about an application and who it misses, then gives renters a notice request and a printable lobby notice.
> - **What the user does:** sees that notice is mailed to owners on the assessment roll within 120 m, and that buildings with 7 or more units are only asked to post it. Taps "I want to be told" to open an email drafting the s.5(8) written request, and can print a dated lobby notice.
> - **Why it's distinctive:** answers "why didn't I hear?" by explaining how notice is distributed.
> - **Grounding:** P040, P044, P225, P228. O. Reg. 545/06 s.5(4), (5), (7), (7.1), (8), (11): https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/060545. Renters 38.7% (18,205 households) and movers 47.1%: https://www.waterloo.ca/media/3o5lqith/2021-census-profiles-city-of-waterloo.pdf. Print paper closed Sept 2023: https://kitchener.ctvnews.ca/four-local-newspapers-will-no-longer-put-out-print-editions-1.6565524.
> - **Tonight:** one application, a "who gets told" card, the email draft and the print notice (about 2 h; 3–4 h with a map). **Fuller version:** every open application.
> - **Main risk:** the team's reading of the regulation clauses must be verified, and a buffer drawn around a point is approximate. Never say a person was or wasn't notified, frame it as access rather than mobilisation, show no owner names, and don't paraphrase appeal rights.

CAT lines 1182–1183 (combination C) place N37 in the "who is left out" bundle and cite "O. Reg. 545/06 s.5(8), O. Reg. 191/11 s.12" as legal routes.

**Result of checking the spec against the law (detail in §2):**
- The subsection numbers in the catalogue are **correct** for the current consolidation of REG: s.5(4), (5), (7), (7.1), (8) and (11) all exist and say what the spec implies.
- The "7 or more units" rule is **correct for REG but needs precise wording**. It is paragraph 7 of s.5(11), a *content* requirement: the mailed notice must include a *request* that the owner post it. Nothing in REG compels the owner to post. **Whether it applies in Waterloo at all is now Unverified** (Correction 4).
- **Correction 1.** s.5(8) only covers the notice *of the public meeting or open house*. Notice of the *decision* (passing of the by-law) is a separate written request under PA s.34(18)(b). The "I want to be told" draft must ask for both.
- **Correction 2.** "Movers 47.1%" is a share of **persons** (base: Total Population 113,415), not households.
- **Correction 3.** The September 2023 print closures reported by CTV concern Metroland community weeklies (named in search results as the Waterloo Chronicle and others). The City publishes notices in *The Record*, which search results say kept printing. Do not use that article to say the notice newspaper closed (see F28).
- **Correction 4 (added in verification).** Waterloo's Official Plan says the City uses the Planning Act's "alternative method" for notice of Official Plan and Zoning By-law amendments (OP12 policy 12.4.2(2), F41). Under PA s.34(14.3) (F19), where an OP's alternative measures are complied with, PA s.34(12) to (14.2) do not apply. Those include s.34(13), the notice that REG s.5 prescribes. So for Waterloo ZBAs the operative notice rules are probably OP12 policies 12.4.3 and 12.4.4, not REG s.5 (Interpretation, needs planning-law review). OP12 still mails only to **owners** within 120 m on the assessment roll (F42), so the renter gap holds. It gives its own request route: email notice "to any persons or public bodies who request to be notified" (F43). It contains **no** 7+ unit posting request (F44). PA s.34(18) decision notice is not displaced by s.34(14.3).

## 5) Risks and what must not be claimed

1. **Never say a person was or wasn't notified.** The rules set minimums. The City may mail more ("properties", F22). SKILL lines 499 and 502–503 apply.
2. **Don't say Waterloo asks 7+ unit buildings to post the notice.** That request is in REG s.5(11) para 7 (F9), but Waterloo's OP alternative measures do not contain it (F44), and REG s.5 may not apply to Waterloo ZBAs (Correction 4). Even where it applies it is a request, not a duty (F9, F10).
3. **Don't present the meeting request as covering the decision.** Decision notice is PA s.34(18)(b) (F15). The draft requests both.
4. **Don't paraphrase appeal rights.** REG prescribes exact statements (F12) and PA s.34(19) was not read. Link out instead. Who may appeal a zoning decision was narrowed in 2022–2024 amendments (Reported, not verified); one more reason not to touch it.
5. **Don't claim the notice newspaper closed.** The 2023 closures concern community weeklies, not The Record (F28, Reported). Newspaper notice is also optional under the OP (F42).
6. **"Movers 47.1%" is persons, not households.** Use it only after checking the base on p.12 (F25), if at all.
7. **The renter figure is a 2021 snapshot.** Always print "2021 Census". The StatCan original was blocked (F27). Do not add district counts (F26).
8. **The 120 m buffer from a point is approximate** (F36). It is measured from the subject land, not a centroid.
9. **Which regime applies is not settled.** Waterloo's OP adopts alternative measures (F41); whether a given file complied with them, and whether REG s.5 still applies to any Waterloo ZBA, is Unverified. Timing also differs (OP 10 days, F45, vs PA 20 days, F17): do not show a notice-period rule. CPP files run under O. Reg. 173/16 (F39). Label the routes "what the City's Official Plan and the provincial rules say", not "what happened on this file".
10. **Decision-maker.** Minor ZBAs may be decided by the Director of Planning (F46). Don't write "Council's decision" generically.
11. **Privacy.** Comments sent to clerkinfo "become part of the public record" (F29), and a 2020 City notice said "names, addresses and comments may be made public" (F33). Warn users that a notice request may also be filed publicly (Unverified either way). Muni stores nothing; it builds a mailto link. Show no owner names, roll data, or the names of staff planners from case pages.
12. **Framing.** Present this as access ("how to be told"), not mobilisation ("tell your neighbours to oppose"). The lobby sheet must be neutral and say it is not the official notice. PLAN line 178 requires that line on the Decision Facts page's print view; apply the same rule here.
13. **Not legal advice.** The interpretation passage (PA-gap-interpretation) must be human-reviewed before release, ideally by someone with planning-law knowledge.

## 6) Verdict: **GROUNDED, with a required reframe** (buildable honestly)

The core claim still stands, and is now stronger: both the provincial regulation and the City's own Official Plan mail notice to **owners** on the assessment roll within 120 m (F3, F42), so renters are not named recipients. Anyone can be told by asking: the OP's email-on-request policy (F43), REG s.5(8) where it applies (F8), and PA s.34(18)(b) for the decision (F15). The renter share (38.7%, 18,205 of 47,040 households) is Observed in the City's 2021 profile. A clerk contact, the City's own description of its publicity channels, and an applicant notice that went to "property owners" are all Observed.

What changed in verification: Waterloo's OP adopts the alternative notice method (F41), so REG s.5 is the provincial default, not necessarily the rule for a Waterloo file. The catalogue's "7 or more units are only asked to post it" line is not supported for Waterloo and should be cut from the copy until a current City notice shows it.

**Before shipping:**
- (a) Change the copy so that the request cites OP 12.4.3(1)(d) and PA s.34(18)(b), with REG s.5(8) as "where it applies".
- (b) Drop the 7+ unit posting line, the newspaper-closure line and the "movers" base.
- (c) Confirm with the City Clerk or Planning which address they want notice requests sent to. Ideally also find one current Waterloo statutory notice and check what it says about decision requests and posting in large buildings.
- (d) Have someone with planning-law knowledge confirm how PA s.34(14.3) and the OP interact for ZBAs, and check the PA s.17 equivalent for joint OPA files.

None of these blocks a one-application prototype that cites the OP and labels the routes as "what the rules say".

Backup idea, if (c)/(d) turn out against it: **N26 Follow the Item.** It reuses the same P040/P225 file-number records and the PA s.34(18) decision-notice route without depending on how Waterloo's mail-out works.
