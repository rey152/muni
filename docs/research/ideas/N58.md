## 1) Spec recap (catalogue, verbatim)

Source: `data/inventory/Muni-new-ideas-catalogue.md` lines 909–915. Status: Observed.

> **N58 · Ask the Record with typed refusals.** A question box that answers only from reviewed records with exact quotes, and otherwise refuses with a stated reason.
> - **What the user does:** asks "Is the King St tower approved?" or "Should I oppose it?" and gets a quoted answer or one of six refusals: not in records; records disagree; exists but not public (P229); not decided yet; opinion or advice; already answered by the City (P205).
> - **Why it's distinctive:** refusal is a designed output, and the City's own answers are used first.
> - **Grounding:** P205, P229, P016, P015. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18418, https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01476.
> - **Tonight:** 6 precomputed questions, rejecting any answer whose quote isn't verbatim (3 h).
> - **Main risk:** the most common hackathon entry, live calls can fail, and free text favours confident writers. Lead with the refusal types.
> - Feasibility 3 · Brief fit 4 · Survives scrutiny 3

Strategy lines in the same catalogue that bear on N58 (Observed, lines 48–52, 60, 73–75):

> 6. **Don't lead the pitch with "AI summaries". Never let an LLM decide status, and avoid an open-ended chatbot.**
> … A-046 s.1.6 requires that non-AI alternatives be considered.
> - Any question box should answer from records only and refuse in typed categories (N58), because judges will ask "should I oppose this?".
> - Precompute and cache outputs, and allow at most one bounded live call during the demo.

> 9. **No points, streaks, leaderboards, outcome predictions or vote aggregation.**

> 13. **Don't encode legal rules (appeal standing, statutory timelines) as authoritative app logic.** … Use dates printed on Waterloo's own notices (P208, P209, P210). Label everything else "summary as of [date], verify".

Scope note (Observed, `Context/Muni-categories-context.md` table rows lines 76–85): N58 is **not** one of the selected Set 69 features (N80, N89, N16, N53, N106, N120, N46, N56, N63, N41). Building it would add scope beyond the approved set. In this dossier's view (analysis, not a source claim), it fits best as a thin layer over Decision Facts (N89) and the Glass Box (N56).

## 4) UX outline

**Input model.** The input is closed by default: question chips per matter, plus a free-text box. Free text goes through a *deterministic* matcher (keyword and identifier rules) that maps it to one of the 24 canonical questions. Two cases always route straight to a refusal:
- Normative cue words ("should", "worth", "oppose", "support") go to `OPINION_OR_ADVOCACY`.
- Future-outcome cues ("will council", "going to pass") go to `OUTCOME_PREDICTION`.

No match gives `UNREVIEWED_QUESTION`. No LLM sits in the answer path; this follows strategy 6, A-046 s.1.6 (F4) and s.1.4 (F8). One optional bounded live call may *rank* chips, but it can never author text.

**Screens and states**
1. **Ask (empty state).** Shows matter chips and "Muni answers only with exact sentences from records it has reviewed, as of {checked_on}". The refusal types are listed up front, as the catalogue advises ("Lead with the refusal types").
   *Does not establish:* that the five matters are everything happening in Waterloo.
2. **Quoted answer.** Shows the verbatim quote in a block, then the source title, publisher, document date, fetched date, locator and a link to the real document URL (D1 fix). It also shows a "What this quote does not establish" line taken from the event's `not_establishes`. Example (Q12): the quote alone, with the line "Does not establish that the property is designated."
   *Does not establish:* currency after the fetched date, or applicability to the asker.
3. **Partial answer.** The quote, plus a typed refusal card for the uncovered part (Q12, Q18).
4. **Typed refusal.** Shows the refusal type name, a fixed sentence and, where one exists, a quoted "what you can do" passage: a delegation quote, a contact quote or an inspection quote. Contacts appear as role mailboxes taken from the record (e.g. heritage@waterloo.ca, clerkinfo@waterloo.ca), never staff names.
   *Does not establish:* that the answer doesn't exist (`UNREVIEWED_QUESTION` and `NOT_IN_RECORDS_CHECKED` both say so explicitly).
5. **Records disagree.** Two quote blocks side by side, each with its date. No winner is chosen.
   *Does not establish:* which is correct, or that either is an error.
6. **Unknown / stale.** If `checked_on` is older than a threshold, or a deadline date in the quote is before today, show "This date has passed (today is {date})". Do not translate that into "closed" or "you can't".
7. **Source unavailable.** If the snapshot is `availability: blocked` (e.g. Region eScribe), refuse with `NOT_IN_RECORDS_CHECKED` and name the access problem.

**Verbatim gate (build time).** Each `answer` passage must be a substring of the snapshot's extracted text after normalizing Unicode quotes and collapsing whitespace, including the P210 split-hyphen "By -law" artifacts. Any other difference fails the build. Case is never normalized, which is why D2 fails. `verbatim_row` passages are checked cell by cell.

**Disclosure.** A one-line AI-use note links to the N56/N57 Glass Box. F7 is the model, but don't claim A-046 applies (see R4).

## 5) Risks and what must not be claimed

- **R1. Absence vs not-checked.** Never answer "No" because nothing matched. `UNREVIEWED_QUESTION` ≠ `NOT_IN_RECORDS_CHECKED` ≠ "didn't happen" (skill l.502).
- **R2. Deadlines have passed.** Today (2026-11-14) is after Nov 6 (objection) and Nov 8 (appeal). Muni must not state whether an appeal was filed or whether By-law 2026-080 is in force. The heritage-notices page (F29) still lists the notice and gives no outcome. The catalogue's N105 risk line flags the same need to re-check.
- **R3. Debt matter has no quotable text.** Do not quote "$7.44M", "3.37%" or "not a borrowing approval" as verbatim until they are captured from the packet. The guide also flags an internal figure conflict (F17).
- **R4. A-046.** Do not say Muni is "A-046 compliant" or that A-046 requires refusals. F9 suggests a third-party tool without City data is outside its scope, and F10 notes the policy says nothing about refusals. s.1.3 binds *staff*; the catalogue's paraphrase overstates it. The catalogue's own N57 line: "designed to meet if City-hosted", never "compliant".
- **R5. Quotes can be outdated or carry source errors.** Keep "filling" and "will shared" as written, marked [sic]. The register (July 2025) predates the by-law (Oct 2026): show both dates and don't "correct" the register.
- **R6. Convenience copy.** The fees PDF states on p.1 that it is a convenience version relied on "at your risk" (F13), so always show that line with fee quotes, even when the quoted row is on a later page.
- **R7. Normative leakage.** The refusal copy must not hint at a side, e.g. "objecting is your right" next to an expired deadline. "How to have your say" may appear only as quoted procedure.
- **R8. Privacy.** EngageWR JSON carries `username` and respondent names. Strip them at ingest, and never index question authors. Notices name staff; show role mailboxes only. A free-text box must not log questions containing addresses or names (A-046 s.1.6 spirit, F5).
- **R9. Arithmetic.** The 2026 and 2027 fee columns invite "it rose 4%". That is a derived claim, not a quote, so leave it out of the answer.
- **R10. Novelty.** The catalogue rates this "the most common hackathon entry". The typed-refusal taxonomy and the verbatim gate are the differentiators. A free-form chatbot demo would erase them.
- **R11. Scope.** N58 is outside Set 69, and adding it needs an explicit decision.
